The Earth Jukebox

Pick a side (a sound style), press play, and hear the satellite days. A playhead sweeps west to east across the frame; each latitude band becomes a note whose loudness follows the data, generated live in your browser.

Start here
  1. Press Play. A playhead sweeps the map, one pass per day.
  2. Drag across the map to hear any single spot.
  3. Turn on Haze mode to focus on haze, or Hear change to hear what shifted between days.
  4. Press Record full sequence to save the soundtrack as an audio file.

The story: why we hear the Earth

Why: maps are silent to blind and low-vision people and hard to read at a glance. Sound lets anyone feel a planet in change. Algorithm: each frame is scanned west to east. Rows are grouped into 12 latitude bands, each tuned to a musical scale. Intensity sets loudness, colour sets the instrument (warm = bright saw, green = soft triangle, blue = pure sine), and local detail sets the pulse rate. Feeling: play a time-lapse and listen for the poles falling silent or the tropics growing louder, the urgency of a warming world heard instead of seen.

How the sonification works

Latitude → pitch (north is higher, 12 bands on a musical scale). Data value → loudness and brightness. Longitude → time. Colour → instrument (custom frames). Image detail → rhythm. Several frames → a time sequence. Haze mode sonifies an estimated haze map. Hear change plays the difference between two days. Sides A to F are six sound styles (scale and timbre) applied to the same day frames: warm major, glass bells, soft strings, dark minor, airy drift and deep dorian.

Accessibility

Everything is audible without sight, each frame has a generated text description, and day changes are announced to screen readers (voice announcements are optional). There is a high-contrast mode, large touch targets on phones, and reduced-motion support.

Keyboard shortcuts

Space: play or pause · ← →: scrub · N / P: next or previous frame · H: haze mode · C: hear change · T: time-lapse · R: record · ?: this list

Sample imagery: NASA Worldview satellite true-colour frames over South Asia, three consecutive days, cropped to a 2:1 strip. Sides A to F change only the sound style. The data is always the loaded satellite frames. To use other imagery, load your own frames with the file picker.
